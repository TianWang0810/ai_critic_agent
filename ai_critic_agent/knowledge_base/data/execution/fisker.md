# Fisker: EV Manufacturing Startup Stranded by Market Slowdown and Capital Gap

## Core Failed Assumption
**"Capital-intensive EV manufacturing startups can bridge funding gaps when the market is hot."**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: Fisker Inc.
- **Founded**: 2016
- **Bankruptcy**: 2024
- **Total Funding**: ~$1 billion+
- **Founders**: Henrik Fisker
- **Category**: execution

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
Fisker believed that EV market enthusiasm would keep capital markets open long enough to fund the production ramp of the Ocean SUV, and that an asset-light contract manufacturing model would reduce the capital intensity of scaling.

**Specific Claims:**
- Contract manufacturing (via Magna) eliminates the capital requirement of a Gigafactory
- EV demand growth will sustain investor appetite through the ramp period
- Design differentiation is sufficient to capture premium EV market share

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
EV consumer demand slowed sharply in 2023-2024 as interest rates rose and EV tax credit complexity limited adoption. Fisker's attempted manufacturing partnership with Nissan collapsed. Without an OEM deal, Fisker could not fund production independently. Filed Chapter 11 in June 2024 with ~$1B in liabilities.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Vehicles Delivered**: ~10,000 Ocean SUVs before bankruptcy
- **Filed Bankruptcy**: June 2024
- **Nissan Deal**: Collapsed in early 2024
- **Liabilities at Filing**: ~$1 billion
- **Stock Price**: Fell from $18 at SPAC peak to near zero

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: Contract Manufacturing Model Could Not Bridge the Capital Gap When Markets Closed
Asset-light EV manufacturing still requires capital-intensive supplier and distribution infrastructure.

**Evidence:**
- Ocean SUV required massive working capital for parts and assembly despite Magna contract
- Dealer inventory built up with no retail demand to absorb it
- Nissan OEM partnership — critical to next model — failed in final negotiations
- Interest rate environment shut off SPAC-era capital that had funded the ramp assumption

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "Asset-light manufacturing is still manufacturing; the capital requirements are deferred, not eliminated."

**Red Flags:**
- Business plan assumes continued access to growth capital through a multi-year ramp
- Contract manufacturer relationship has not been finalized or stress-tested
- Demand projections built on peak-market conditions without sensitivity analysis

### Counterargument Template
> "Fisker illustrates that contract manufacturing does not eliminate the capital intensity of EV production — it restructures it. Working capital for components, warranty reserves, and distribution infrastructure still require hundreds of millions regardless of who operates the factory. When market conditions shift and growth capital dries up simultaneously, manufacturing startups have no margin of safety. Any EV startup must demonstrate a path to cash flow positive production before the next capital raise, not after it."

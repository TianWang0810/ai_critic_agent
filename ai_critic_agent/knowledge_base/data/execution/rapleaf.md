# Rapleaf: Behavioral Surveillance Data Business Shut Down by Press and Senate

## Core Failed Assumption
**"Data businesses built on behavioral surveillance will scale before regulators or press attention catch up."**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: Rapleaf
- **Founded**: 2005
- **Shut Down / Pivoted**: 2011
- **Total Funding**: ~$17 million
- **Founders**: Auren Hoffman
- **Category**: execution

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
Rapleaf believed it could aggregate behavioral data scraped from social networks, public records, and purchase history to build consumer profiles sold to marketers — and that the business would reach defensible scale before data privacy became a regulated concern.

**Specific Claims:**
- Consumer profile aggregation is legal under existing data laws
- Marketers' demand for behavioral targeting data justifies aggressive data collection
- Scale of profiles creates a data moat competitors cannot replicate

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
A 2010 Wall Street Journal investigation exposed Rapleaf as transmitting user data — including Facebook IDs — to ad networks without meaningful consumer consent. Senate investigations followed. Advertisers pulled back. The company rebranded as TowerData and pivoted away from the consumer tracking business.

**Key Metrics:**
- **WSJ Exposure**: October 2010 — "The Web's Gold Mine: Your Secrets"
- **Senate Inquiry**: Commerce Committee investigation 2010-2011
- **Outcome**: Full rebrand to TowerData; consumer profile business shut down
- **Funding**: ~$17 million raised prior to collapse

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: Surveillance Business Model Assumed Regulatory Tolerance It Did Not Have
Congressional attention and press exposure are irreversible once they reach scale.

**Evidence:**
- Facebook IDs transmitted to ad networks in violation of Facebook's terms of service
- No clear user consent mechanism for profile aggregation from scraped sources
- Senate Commerce Committee sent formal inquiries demanding business practice disclosure
- Advertiser clients quietly terminated contracts after public exposure

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "Data businesses without a consent foundation become regulatory targets, not defensible assets."

**Red Flags:**
- Revenue model depends on data collection that users have not affirmatively consented to
- Business relies on platform scraping that violates terms of service of data sources
- No privacy policy or consent mechanism proportionate to the sensitivity of data collected

### Counterargument Template
> "Rapleaf demonstrates that behavioral surveillance businesses face a race they cannot win: press and regulatory attention scales faster than the business moat. Consumer data collected without consent is a liability, not an asset, once disclosure occurs. Privacy regulation (GDPR, CCPA) has since codified what the Rapleaf case made clear commercially: data businesses must be built on consent, not extraction. Any startup whose data advantage depends on collecting consumer behavior without clear opt-in should be treated as a regulatory time bomb."

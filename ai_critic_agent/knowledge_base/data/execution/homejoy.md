# Homejoy: Marketplace Retention Collapse in Home Services

## Core Failed Assumption
**"Marketplace model creates defensible retention once customers experience the service."**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: Homejoy
- **Founded**: 2012
- **Shut Down**: 2015
- **Total Funding**: $65.9 million
- **Founders**: Adora Cheung, Aaron Cheung
- **Category**: execution

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
Homejoy believed that once customers experienced a quality home cleaning through the platform, they would return exclusively through Homejoy rather than booking the cleaner directly.

**Specific Claims:**
- Service quality would create habit-forming repeat bookings
- Platform convenience would outweigh any savings from off-platform booking
- Geographic expansion would compound network effects and improve supply quality

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
Customers discovered that booking cleaners directly was cheaper and equally convenient. The platform's retention collapsed as cleaners and customers routinely circumvented it. Simultaneously, worker misclassification lawsuits threatened the independent contractor model that kept costs low.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Customer Retention Rate**: Below 25%
- **Cities Expanded To**: 30+ before fixing core retention
- **Lawsuits**: Multiple worker misclassification suits filed
- **Time to Shutdown**: ~3 years post-launch

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: No Structural Lock-In Against Off-Platform Booking
The marketplace created the relationship but provided no mechanism to own it.

**Evidence:**
- Cleaners openly shared contact information with satisfied customers
- Platform fees gave both sides incentive to transact off-platform
- Expansion to 30+ cities amplified the retention problem rather than solving it
- Misclassification lawsuits removed the cost advantage of the 1099 model

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "Marketplace retention requires structural lock-in, not just satisfaction."

**Red Flags:**
- No in-platform communication tools that prevent off-platform contact
- Unit economics that make direct booking financially rational for both sides
- Aggressive city expansion before proving retention in pilot markets

### Counterargument Template
> "Homejoy demonstrates that customer satisfaction alone does not create marketplace retention when the economics of disintermediation are favorable to both parties. A marketplace that introduces supply and demand without owning the payment relationship or communication layer is effectively a subsidized introduction service. Expansion before solving retention compounds the problem at scale, as each new market inherits the same structural leak. Any home-services marketplace must show evidence of structural switching costs — not just satisfaction scores — before claiming durable unit economics."

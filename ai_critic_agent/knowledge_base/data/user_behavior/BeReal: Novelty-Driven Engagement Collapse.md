# BeReal: Novelty-Driven Engagement Collapse

## Core Failed Assumption
**"Authenticity-based novelty is sufficient to sustain long-term social engagement"**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: BeReal
- **Founded**: 2020
- **Status**: Rapid growth then decline
- **Total Funding**: $90M+
- **Founders**: Alexis Barreyat
- **Category**: user_behavior

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
BeReal believed limiting posts to one random daily moment would create durable engagement driven by authenticity.

**Specific Claims:**
- Authenticity beats curation
- Daily habit would form naturally
- Novelty would not wear off

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
User engagement dropped sharply once novelty faded. Many users ignored notifications or churned.

**Key Metrics:**
- **DAU Decline**: >60% within a year
- **Notification Ignore Rate**: High
- **Retention**: Weak beyond early cohorts

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: User Behavior (Novelty Decay)
Novelty-based mechanics rarely sustain long-term habits.

**Evidence:**
- Engagement peaks tied to virality
- No strong social feedback loops
- Limited intrinsic motivation

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "Novelty is not a retention strategy."

**Red Flags:**
- Single-mechanic engagement
- Reliance on virality
- Weak social reinforcement

### Counterargument Template
> "BeReal shows that authenticity-driven novelty spikes usage but collapses without deeper habit formation."

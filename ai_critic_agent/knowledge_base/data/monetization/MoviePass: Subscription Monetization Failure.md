# MoviePass: Subscription Monetization Failure

## Core Failed Assumption
**"Negative unit economics can be fixed through scale"**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: MoviePass
- **Founded**: 2011
- **Shut Down**: 2019
- **Total Funding**: $300+ million
- **Founders**: Stacy Spikes, Hamet Watt
- **Category**: monetization

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
MoviePass believed that aggressive pricing would force theaters into revenue-sharing partnerships, allowing scale to reverse negative margins.

**Specific Claims:**
- $9.95/month unlimited sustainable
- User behavior would normalize
- Scale creates negotiating leverage

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
Heavy users maximized consumption, destroying margins. Theater partners never renegotiated.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Cost/User**: $15–$20/month
- **Revenue/User**: $9.95
- **Burn Rate**: ~$40M/month

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: Monetization Fantasy
Pricing below marginal cost without control mechanisms guarantees collapse.

**Evidence:**
- Unlimited usage abuse
- No contractual leverage
- Rapid cash burn

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "You cannot outgrow negative unit economics."

**Red Flags:**
- Below-cost pricing
- Reliance on hypothetical future leverage
- No usage caps

### Counterargument Template
> "MoviePass scaled to millions of users while losing money on every transaction, proving that scale amplifies losses when unit economics are broken."

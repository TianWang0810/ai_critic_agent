# Friendster: Social Network Timing Failure

## Core Failed Assumption
**"Early adoption guarantees long-term dominance in social networks"**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: Friendster
- **Founded**: 2002
- **Shut Down**: 2011
- **Total Funding**: $5M+
- **Founders**: Jonathan Abrams
- **Category**: timing

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
Friendster believed that being the first major social network would ensure long-term leadership before competitors could emerge.

**Specific Claims:**
- Early user base would lock in network effects
- Scaling challenges could be solved later
- Competitors would not overtake momentum

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
Technical infrastructure failed under growth, users abandoned the platform, and competitors filled the gap.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Page Load Time**: >30 seconds at peak
- **User Churn**: Massive during outages
- **Market Position**: Displaced by MySpace, then Facebook

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: Timing vs Readiness
The market arrived before the company was technically ready.

**Evidence:**
- Frequent site outages
- Slow feature iteration
- User migration during instability

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "Timing advantage evaporates without operational readiness."

**Red Flags:**
- Scaling issues ignored
- Deferred infrastructure investment
- Rapid growth without reliability

### Counterargument Template
> "Friendster shows that being early offers no protection if the product collapses under real usage."

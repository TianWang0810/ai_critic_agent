# Segway: Consumer Transportation Timing Failure

## Core Failed Assumption
**"A revolutionary product will create its own market immediately"**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: Segway
- **Founded**: 1999
- **Product Launch**: 2001
- **Status**: Niche adoption only
- **Category**: timing

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
Segway believed personal electric transport would be instantly adopted and reshape urban mobility.

**Specific Claims:**
- Cities would adapt infrastructure
- Consumers would pay premium prices
- Regulatory approval would follow demand

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
Infrastructure and regulation lagged. The product found only niche use cases.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Projected Sales**: 50K units/year
- **Actual Sales**: Far below projections
- **Main Users**: Tour operators, security

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: Ecosystem Timing
Urban infrastructure and regulations were not ready.

**Evidence:**
- Sidewalk legality issues
- High price point
- Limited everyday use cases

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "Adoption depends on ecosystem readiness, not product novelty."

**Red Flags:**
- Dependence on regulation change
- High upfront pricing
- No fallback use cases

### Counterargument Template
> "Segway demonstrates that revolutionary products still fail when cities, laws, and habits are unprepared."

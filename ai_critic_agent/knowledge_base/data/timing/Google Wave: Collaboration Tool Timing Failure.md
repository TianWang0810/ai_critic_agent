# Google Wave: Collaboration Tool Timing Failure

## Core Failed Assumption
**"Users are ready for radically new collaboration paradigms"**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: Google
- **Founded**: 2009
- **Shut Down**: 2012
- **Category**: timing

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
Google believed users would quickly adopt a fundamentally new way to communicate and collaborate.

**Specific Claims:**
- Email was obsolete
- Users would learn new mental models
- Feature richness would drive adoption

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
Users were overwhelmed and reverted to simpler tools.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Adoption Rate**: Low post-launch
- **Retention**: Poor beyond early adopters
- **Product Complexity**: High

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: Behavioral Timing Gap
User readiness lagged technical possibility.

**Evidence:**
- Steep learning curve
- Poor onboarding comprehension
- Preference for familiar tools

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "Behavioral change lags technical capability."

**Red Flags:**
- Radical UX without migration path
- Feature overload
- Dismissal of habit inertia

### Counterargument Template
> "Google Wave shows that even powerful tools fail when users aren’t ready to change how they think."

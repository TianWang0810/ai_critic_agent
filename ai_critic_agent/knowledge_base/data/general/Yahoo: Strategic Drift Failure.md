# Yahoo: Strategic Drift Failure

## Core Failed Assumption
**"A strong brand can remain relevant without strategic focus"**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: Yahoo
- **Founded**: 1994
- **Acquired**: 2017 (Verizon)
- **Peak Valuation**: $125B (2000)
- **Category**: general

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
Yahoo believed brand recognition and portal dominance allowed it to experiment broadly without committing to clear strategic direction.

**Specific Claims:**
- Optionality beats focus
- Brand attracts users regardless of product quality
- Market leadership persists by default

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
Yahoo missed or misexecuted nearly every major platform shift.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Missed Opportunities**: Search, social, mobile
- **Market Share**: Continuous decline
- **Exit Valuation**: ~$4.5B core business

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: Strategic Diffusion
Lack of focus prevented decisive execution.

**Evidence:**
- Failed acquisitions
- Slow innovation
- Leadership churn

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "Focus beats optionality."

**Red Flags:**
- Too many parallel bets
- No core product clarity
- Brand-first thinking

### Counterargument Template
> "Yahoo’s decline shows that brand strength erodes rapidly without strategic focus."

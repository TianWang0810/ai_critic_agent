# Juicero: Non-Existent Market for Premium Juice Hardware

## Core Failed Assumption
**"A large consumer market exists for expensive, single-purpose kitchen hardware"**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: Juicero
- **Founded**: 2013
- **Shut Down**: 2017
- **Total Funding**: $120 million
- **Founders**: Doug Evans
- **Category**: market

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
Juicero believed health-conscious consumers wanted a premium, connected juice appliance and would pay hundreds of dollars for marginal convenience gains.

**Specific Claims:**
- Consumers value cold-press precision
- Premium pricing signals quality
- Market size supports mass adoption

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
Consumers did not value the hardware enough to justify its cost. Demand collapsed once the true value proposition was exposed.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Hardware Price**: $699 → $399 (price cut)
- **Repeat Usage**: Extremely low
- **Market Adoption**: Minimal beyond early adopters

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: Market Size Illusion
The intersection of users willing to pay and users needing the product was tiny.

**Evidence:**
- Manual alternatives sufficient
- Low willingness to pay for single-use devices
- Rapid post-launch demand collapse

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "A market defined by aspiration rather than behavior rarely exists."

**Red Flags:**
- Premium pricing without necessity
- Market defined by lifestyle branding
- Weak substitute analysis

### Counterargument Template
> "Juicero shows that just because a user segment exists does not mean a paying market exists."

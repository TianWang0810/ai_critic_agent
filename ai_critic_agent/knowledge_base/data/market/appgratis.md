# AppGratis: App Discovery Business Removed from App Store Overnight

## Core Failed Assumption
**"Single-platform distribution that depends on App Store goodwill is a durable business model."**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: AppGratis
- **Founded**: 2010
- **Shut Down**: 2013
- **Total Funding**: ~$15 million
- **Founders**: Simon Dawlat
- **Category**: market

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
AppGratis believed it could build a sustainable app discovery and daily deals business by directing iOS users to discounted and free apps, with the App Store as its primary distribution and the source of its audience.

**Specific Claims:**
- App discovery is a durable market need Apple will not serve directly
- 12 million downloads validates long-term consumer demand for curated app discovery
- Apple's continued approval indicates tacit acceptance of the business model

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
Apple removed AppGratis from the App Store in April 2013, citing violations of App Store guidelines: specifically, directing users away from App Store discovery mechanisms and using push notifications to promote third-party apps for compensation. The removal was without prior warning. The company laid off most of its team and never recovered its scale.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Downloads Before Removal**: 12 million+
- **Removal Date**: April 2013 — without prior warning
- **Reason Given**: App Store guideline violations (sections 2.25 and 5.6)
- **Team Size at Removal**: ~45 employees
- **Outcome**: Near-total business collapse within months

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: The Entire Business Operated at Apple's Discretion Without Contractual Protection
Platform-dependent businesses can be eliminated by a single policy decision with no recourse.

**Evidence:**
- Apple cited guidelines that existed at the time of AppGratis's original approval — approval was never a guarantee of continued access
- AppGratis monetized by directing user attention away from Apple's own discovery mechanisms (App Store Featured)
- No appeal process or grace period was given; removal was immediate and permanent
- The business had no distribution channel independent of the platform it competed with

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "A business that lives inside a platform operates at the platform owner's discretion, not its own."

**Red Flags:**
- 90%+ of revenue or distribution flows through a single platform
- Business model competes with or substitutes for a feature the platform has strategic incentive to own
- No multi-platform or direct distribution channel exists as a fallback

### Counterargument Template
> "AppGratis illustrates the fundamental fragility of building a business whose existence depends on platform goodwill. Apple's removal decision was consistent, predictable in retrospect, and perfectly rational — AppGratis competed with Apple's own curation and monetized attention Apple considered its own. Any startup that depends on a single platform for distribution must answer: what happens when the platform adds this feature natively, or decides our business model conflicts with their guidelines? Without a credible alternative distribution path, the business has a single point of failure it cannot control."

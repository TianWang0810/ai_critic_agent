# Aereo: Supreme Court Rules TV Streaming Startup's Business Model Illegal

## Core Failed Assumption
**"Individual-antenna legal theory creates a defensible business model in broadcast retransmission."**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: Aereo
- **Founded**: 2012
- **Shut Down**: 2014
- **Total Funding**: $97 million
- **Founders**: Chet Kanojia
- **Investors**: Barry Diller (IAC)
- **Category**: market

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
Aereo believed that assigning each subscriber a tiny individual antenna in its data centers — rather than a shared broadcast retransmission system — created a legal distinction from cable companies, which must pay broadcasters retransmission fees. This would allow Aereo to stream live TV for $8/month without licensing costs.

**Specific Claims:**
- Individual antenna assignment is legally equivalent to a private over-the-air antenna
- Lower courts confirming the theory validated the long-term legal risk
- Broadcaster licensing fees are an incumbency tax, not a legal requirement at Aereo's scale

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
The US Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in ABC v. Aereo (June 2014) that Aereo performed public transmissions equivalent to cable retransmission, requiring it to pay broadcaster licensing fees. The fees made the $8/month model uneconomical. Aereo shut down within months and filed for bankruptcy.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Supreme Court Decision**: ABC v. Aereo, June 2014, 6-3 against Aereo
- **Shutdown**: Filed for bankruptcy August 2014
- **Subscribers at Peak**: ~50,000
- **Total Raised**: $97 million
- **Business Model Price**: $8/month — unviable with licensing fees added

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: Supreme Court Rejected the Legal Theory the Entire Business Model Depended On
A business model built on a novel legal interpretation carries catastrophic binary risk at the Supreme Court level.

**Evidence:**
- Lower court wins created false confidence in a theory the Supreme Court majority rejected
- Broadcasters (ABC, NBC, CBS, Fox) coordinated a unified legal challenge from day one
- No fallback revenue model existed that was viable if the antenna theory failed
- At $8/month, adding any licensing fee made the product uncompetitive with cable

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "A business model built on a novel legal theory is a legal bet, not a market insight."

**Red Flags:**
- Business model viability depends entirely on one legal interpretation surviving appellate review
- Well-capitalized industry incumbents have aligned financial interest in defeating the legal theory
- No fallback unit economics if the legal theory is overturned

### Counterargument Template
> "Aereo demonstrates that building a business on a novel legal theory — even one validated by lower courts — is a binary existential risk rather than a competitive advantage. Broadcasters had both the financial resources and the strategic incentive to litigate to the Supreme Court level, making the legal outcome deterministic over a long enough time horizon. Any startup whose entire margin structure depends on avoiding regulatory fees that incumbents pay should be evaluated as a legal arbitrage, not a technology business — and legal arbitrages close."

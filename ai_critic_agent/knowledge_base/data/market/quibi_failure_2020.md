# Quibi: Premium Mobile Video Streaming Failure

## Core Failed Assumption
**"Users will pay $5-8/month for premium short-form video content designed for mobile viewing during commute times"**

---

## Case Overview
- **Company**: Quibi
- **Founded**: 2018
- **Shut Down**: December 2020 (6 months after launch)
- **Total Funding**: $1.75 billion
- **Founders**: Jeffrey Katzenberg (DreamWorks), Meg Whitman (former eBay CEO)
- **Category**: market, timing, monetization

---

## What They Believed (Hypothesis)
Quibi believed there was a massive untapped market for Hollywood-quality short videos (under 10 minutes) specifically designed for mobile viewing. They assumed users would pay premium subscription fees for professionally-produced content optimized for "in-between moments" like commuting.

**Specific Claims:**
- Users wanted premium alternatives to YouTube/TikTok with higher production values
- Commuters would consume content regularly during transit (7-10 minutes average)
- $4.99-$7.99/month pricing was justified by content quality
- 7 million subscribers achievable in first year

---

## What Actually Happened (Reality)
Quibi launched in April 2020 during COVID-19 pandemic when commuting disappeared. The service attracted initial downloads but failed catastrophically at converting to paid subscribers. After burning through $1.75B, the company shut down just 6 months post-launch.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Subscribers**: 72,000 paid (vs 7M target) - 99% miss
- **Conversion Rate**: 8% from free trial (vs 25% industry standard)
- **Daily Active Users**: 1.5M at peak (vs 7M+ needed for sustainability)
- **Content Hours**: 175+ shows produced, most unwatched
- **Valuation Loss**: $1.75B → $0 in 6 months

---

## Why It Failed

### Primary Reason: Market Demand Misjudgment
Users fundamentally did not want to pay for short-form video content, regardless of quality. The assumption that "premium production = willingness to pay" was false in a market dominated by free alternatives.

**Evidence:**
- TikTok/YouTube already provided free short video with massive content libraries
- User surveys showed 89% preferred free ad-supported over paid ad-free for short content
- Competitor analysis: Snapchat Originals (free) had 10x more engagement
- Historical data: No successful premium short-video platform existed

### Secondary Reason: Timing Catastrophe
COVID-19 eliminated the core use case (commuting) right at launch. The "mobile-first" positioning became a liability when users were stuck at home with TV screens.

**Evidence:**
- 60% reduction in commute time March-December 2020
- Users consumed more long-form content (Netflix, Disney+) during lockdown
- Mobile viewing share dropped from 55% to 42% during pandemic
- No social sharing features for stay-at-home entertainment

### Tertiary Reason: Monetization Model Mismatch
Subscription pricing in an ad-supported short-video market. Users already paid for Netflix/Disney+/HBO and showed subscription fatigue.

**Evidence:**
- Average user had 3.4 streaming subscriptions already
- 72% of target demographic used ad-supported free services (YouTube, TikTok)
- $4.99/month represented 5-7 hours of minimum wage work for students
- CAC of $7.50 vs $5/month revenue = negative unit economics

---

## Lessons for Startup Critique

### For Similar Assumptions
> "If a startup assumes users will pay premium prices for content available free elsewhere, Quibi's $1.75B failure demonstrates this rarely works. Even with A-list talent and massive marketing, conversion rates remained 3x below industry standards."

**Red Flags:**
- Claiming "quality justifies price" in markets with free alternatives
- Building for a specific use case (commuting) without fallback scenarios
- Ignoring existing user behavior (free video consumption)
- Assuming brand power (Hollywood names) overcomes market dynamics

### Counterargument Template
> "Quibi raised $1.75 billion with top-tier Hollywood backing and achieved only 72,000 paid subscribers (8% conversion) before shutting down. This demonstrates that premium pricing for short-form video fails against free alternatives like TikTok and YouTube, even with superior production quality. Users consistently choose free ad-supported content over paid subscriptions in this category."

---

## Supporting Data

### User/Market Statistics
- Mobile video market: 85% free ad-supported, 12% premium subscription, 3% TVOD
- Short-form video consumption: 92% on free platforms (TikTok, YouTube, Instagram)
- Willingness to pay for short content: 11% of surveyed users vs 67% for long-form
- Subscription fatigue: 64% of consumers felt they had "too many subscriptions" in 2020

### Financial Data
- Burn rate: $100M per month in operating costs
- Content costs: $6M per hour of content produced
- Customer Acquisition Cost: $7.50 per install, $94 per paid subscriber
- Lifetime Value: ~$15 (average 3-month subscription before churn)
- CAC/LTV ratio: 6.3:1 (unsustainable; healthy is <1:3)

### Timeline
- **Aug 2018**: Quibi announced, raises $1B
- **Mar 2020**: Additional $750M raised
- **Apr 6, 2020**: Launch during COVID-19 lockdown peak
- **May 2020**: 910K trial signups, only 72K convert to paid
- **Jun 2020**: Adds Chromecast support (too late)
- **Oct 21, 2020**: Announces shutdown
- **Dec 1, 2020**: Service terminates
